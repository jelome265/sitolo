# Sitolo — Distribution, Channel & Market Access Strategy

**Status:** Commercial operating-model control document  
**Date:** 2026-09-22  
**Market:** Malawi first  
**Primary question:** How can Sitolo reach merchants beyond founder-led acquisition?

---

## 1. Strategic Question

The commercial challenge is not:

> Can a Duka use Sitolo?

It is:

> Can Sitolo acquire and onboard thousands of merchants without physically visiting each one?

Distribution is therefore a first-class business capability.

---

## 2. Coverage Model

```text
NATIONAL
   ↓
REGION
   ↓
DISTRICT
   ↓
TRADING CENTRE
   ↓
LOCAL BUSINESS NETWORK
   ↓
MERCHANT
```

The organization should operate from the assumption that urban acquisition and rural/distributed acquisition are different operating problems.

---

## 3. Channel Hypotheses

Potential channels to validate:

- direct digital acquisition;
- customer referrals;
- accountants/bookkeepers;
- wholesalers/distributors;
- mobile-money/telecom agent ecosystems;
- merchant associations;
- local technology agents;
- reseller/dealer organizations;
- device retailers;
- community business networks;
- enterprise partnerships.

No channel is assumed to scale until contribution economics are demonstrated.

---

## 4. Channel Matrix

| Channel | Reach | Trust transfer | Onboarding ability | Cost risk | Key validation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Digital/self-service | potentially broad | low-medium | high | low variable / paid acquisition risk | CAC + activation |
| Referral | networked | high | medium | incentive cost | referred retention |
| Accountant | concentrated business network | high | high | partner commission | qualified-account yield |
| Wholesaler | highly relevant merchant touchpoints | high | high | commercial dependency | activation by cohort |
| Mobile-money agent | geographically distributed | medium-high | medium | commission/controls | merchant conversion |
| Technology agent | localized support | medium | high | labour cost | contribution |
| Device retailer | hardware-linked | medium | medium | bundle complexity | software attach |
| Association | concentrated audience | medium-high | medium | event/outreach cost | paid conversion |
| Enterprise partner | limited but high value | high | assisted | high sales cycle | ACV/contribution |

---

## 5. Channel Selection Rules

Select a channel only when it demonstrates evidence across:

```text
REACH
+
QUALIFICATION
+
ACTIVATION
+
PAYMENT
+
RETENTION
+
CONTRIBUTION
```

Raw lead volume is insufficient.

---

## 6. Geographic Rollout

Do not activate the whole country operationally on day one.

Use controlled clusters:

```text
CITY / INITIAL CLUSTER
→ ADJACENT DISTRICT
→ MULTI-DISTRICT
→ NATIONAL
```

Each cluster should measure:

- leads;
- activation;
- travel/support burden;
- payment method;
- retention;
- partner performance;
- network/device problems.

---

## 7. Referral Model

A referral program may reward:

- customer;
- partner;
- reseller;
- agent.

Reward design must avoid:

- fake accounts;
- duplicate identities;
- self-referrals;
- churn-and-rejoin gaming;
- commission on unpaid subscriptions.

Commission should normally be tied to verified economic events.

---

## 8. Accountant Channel

Accountants can influence multiple businesses.

Potential model:

```text
ACCOUNTANT
→ INTRODUCES MERCHANT
→ MERCHANT ACTIVATES
→ ACCOUNTANT SEES PERMITTED SUMMARY
```

Do not expose customer data merely because an accountant referred the account.

Accountant access must remain organization-authorized and permissioned.

---

## 9. Wholesaler / Distributor Channel

Wholesalers already interact with retailers around:

- purchasing;
- replenishment;
- credit;
- product knowledge.

Sitolo should validate whether these relationships can support merchant acquisition.

Potential motion:

```text
WHOLESALER
→ DEMONSTRATION / REFERRAL
→ MERCHANT
→ MOBILE ACTIVATION
→ FIRST SALE
```

Do not require wholesalers to become technical support departments.

---

## 10. Mobile-Money / Telecom Agent Channel

Agent networks can potentially solve geographic reach.

However, the commercial role must be explicit.

An agent may be:

- referral source;
- onboarding assister;
- payment facilitator through an authorized arrangement;
- support escalation point.

These are materially different responsibilities.

Do not bundle them together without economic and compliance analysis.

---

## 11. Technology Agent Model

A local agent can assist with:

- installation;
- basic setup;
- training;
- device configuration;
- first inventory entry;
- first sale;
- support escalation.

The agent must never receive unrestricted production credentials.

Support access follows the existing security/support-access model.

---

## 12. Reseller Model

A reseller sells Sitolo as a product/service.

Required capabilities:

- lead attribution;
- commission calculation;
- customer ownership;
- support boundaries;
- termination;
- fraud controls.

The reseller must not own the customer's organization identity.

---

## 13. Channel Conflict

A single merchant may be touched by:

- direct sales;
- referral;
- accountant;
- wholesaler;
- agent.

Attribution rules must prevent disputes.

Possible model:

- first qualified source;
- last assist source;
- primary commercial source;
- explicit partner override.

The chosen model must be simple enough to reconcile.

---

## 14. Partner Economics

Per partner:

```text
PARTNER CONTRIBUTION
=
DIRECT REVENUE ATTRIBUTED
−
COMMISSION
−
TRAINING COST
−
SUPPORT COST
−
INCENTIVES
−
TRAVEL / FIELD COST
```

Measure contribution, not gross bookings.

---

## 15. Partner Quality

Track:

- qualified leads;
- activation;
- paid conversion;
- 90-day retention;
- support incidents;
- fraud rate;
- commission cost;
- revenue per referred account;
- contribution;
- partner concentration.

A channel that produces 40% of customers but 90% of disputes is a material dependency.

---

## 16. Channel Concentration Risk

Do not allow a single partner to control a large share of acquisition without contingency planning.

Monitor:

```PARTNER SHARE OF NEW ACCOUNTS
PARTNER SHARE OF REVENUE
PARTNER SHARE OF SUPPORT LOAD
```

Set internal concentration alerts.

---

## 17. Village / Trading-Centre Operating Pattern

The physical distribution model should be hub-and-spoke.

```text
DISTRICT HUB
  |
  +-- AGENT
  +-- ACCOUNTANT
  +-- WHOLESALER
  +-- LOCAL PARTNER
       |
     DUKAS
```

The hub's role is to reduce the marginal cost of merchant acquisition and support.

---

## 18. Digital + Physical Hybrid

The strongest low-cost model may be:

```text
DIGITAL DISCOVERY
+
LOCAL TRUSTED ASSISTANCE
+
SELF-SERVICE DAILY USE
```

Do not create a permanently labour-intensive field model for a low-ARPU product.

Field effort should be concentrated on:

- acquisition;
- first value;
- high-value accounts;
- channel enablement;
- escalations.

---

## 19. Channel Onboarding Standard

Every new partner needs:

- written role;
- commercial terms;
- eligibility;
- training;
- attribution;
- support boundary;
- data boundary;
- security expectations;
- fraud controls;
- termination conditions.

No “informal partner” should have privileged production access.

---

## 20. Channel Experiment Design

For each experiment define:

- geography;
- partner cohort;
- channel ID;
- account eligibility;
- expected lead volume;
- activation target;
- payment target;
- retention window;
- CAC formula;
- contribution formula;
- stop rule.

Use the existing commercial validation framework.

---

## 21. Distribution Metrics

### Acquisition

- qualified leads/channel;
- CAC/channel;
- conversion.

### Activation

- first sale;
- time to first value;
- first-week activity.

### Retention

- 30/90/180-day retention.

### Economics

- partner commission;
- support cost;
- travel;
- contribution;
- CAC payback.

### Geographic

- new accounts by district;
- partner coverage;
- support response time;
- device/network issue density.

---

## 22. Rural Readiness Gate

Before expanding a channel into rural/high-distribution areas, verify:

- payment method works;
- onboarding works with constrained connectivity;
- support escalation exists;
- device requirements are realistic;
- offline recovery is reliable;
- partner economics are positive or intentionally subsidized;
- fraud controls are active.

---

## 23. Market Access Anti-Patterns

Reject:

- “go nationwide” before a repeatable channel exists;
- counting partner signups as distribution;
- paying commission on leads instead of economic outcomes;
- relying on one distributor;
- giving partners excessive data access;
- making agents responsible for technical operations they cannot support;
- field-sales economics that exceed Duka contribution.

---

## 24. Final Distribution Contract

```text
CHANNEL REACH
→ QUALIFIED CUSTOMER
→ FIRST VALUE
→ PAID ACCOUNT
→ RETAINED ACCOUNT
→ POSITIVE CONTRIBUTION
```

The commercial machine is not scalable because it reaches merchants. It is scalable when it reaches them repeatedly at acceptable contribution cost.
