# Sitolo — Commercial Corpus Audit

**Status:** Canonical commercial documentation audit contract
**Date:** 2026-09-22
**Scope:** All commercial strategy, validation, pricing, packaging, lifecycle, distribution, economics, trust, regulatory, and market-expansion documentation in the current commercial documentation tranche
**Market:** Malawi first; regional expansion only after evidence gates
**Primary purpose:** establish whether the commercial corpus is internally coherent, decision-ready, evidence-disciplined, and safe to use as an implementation input

---

## 1. Executive Decision

The Sitolo commercial corpus is intentionally broad because Sitolo is intended to serve a continuum from neighborhood Duka through larger SMEs, multi-branch operators, and enterprise organizations.

The corpus must therefore be audited as a **system of commercial contracts**, not as a collection of independent essays.

The audit has five objectives:

1. eliminate contradictory definitions;
2. establish one authority for every commercial decision;
3. distinguish facts, assumptions, hypotheses, scenarios, forecasts, experiments, and approved decisions;
4. ensure every commercially material assumption can be validated or falsified;
5. prevent commercial documentation from silently becoming an unvalidated engineering requirement.

The governing principle is:

> **Broad product scope does not require broad initial validation. The product may serve Duka through enterprise while each commercial claim is validated against the segment, channel, price, workflow, and economic conditions to which it actually applies.**

```text

        ↓
SIGNED PROVIDER / PARTNER CONTRACT
        ↓
APPROVED BUSINESS DECISION RECORD
        ↓
COMMERCIAL VALIDATION PLAN
        ↓
BUSINESS MODEL DESIGN
        ↓
COMMERCIAL OPERATING MODEL
        ↓
SPECIALIZED COMMERCIAL DOCUMENTS
        ↓
MARKETING / PRESENTATION COPY
```

Technical, security, domain, database, API, synchronization, deployment, and operational contracts remain authoritative for their respective technical boundaries.

A lower-level document MUST NOT silently redefine a higher-level commercial concept.

If two documents conflict, the conflict is a defect until resolved. It must not be resolved by whichever document was edited most recently.

# 5. Semantic Classification Contract

Every material commercial statement MUST belong to one of these classes.

| Class | Meaning | May drive implementation? | Evidence required |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Fact | verified external/internal observation | Yes, where relevant | source or reproducible observation |
| Assumption | explicit unresolved belief used for planning | No by itself | must be tracked |
| Hypothesis | falsifiable proposition | No by itself | experiment |
| Scenario | conditional model case | No | assumptions + model |
| Forecast | time-bound expectation based on stated methodology | No as truth | model + assumptions |
| Experiment result | observed test outcome | Yes for validated decision | raw/derived evidence |
| Approved decision | formally promoted business rule | Yes | decision record |
| Requirement | engineering/product obligation | Yes | authoritative requirement source |

The following language is prohibited unless its evidence class supports it:

- “customers will”;
- “Dukas cannot pay”;
- “enterprise will pay”;
- “the market needs”;
- “CAC is”;
- “PMF exists”;
- “this channel works”;
- “this price is correct”;
- “customers prefer mobile”;
- “this feature causes retention”.

Prefer:

- “planning assumption”;
- “hypothesis”; 
- “observed result”; 
- “validated under experiment X”; 
- “scenario”; 
- “pending evidence”.

# 6. Tier Consistency Audit

Sitolo's commercial tier model is:

```text
DUKA
  ↓
SME
  ↓
GROWTH
  ↓
MULTI-BRANCH
  ↓
ENTERPRISE
```

These are packaging/operating-complexity tiers within one Sitolo platform.

## 6.1 Duka

Canonical commercial characteristics:

- normally one operating location;
- small operational team;
- mobile-first workflow;
- high price sensitivity;
- low-cost onboarding requirement;
- simple day-to-day operational needs.

Primary workflow:

```text
SELL → STOCK → MONEY → TODAY
```

## 6.2 SME

Canonical characteristics:

- more users and operational activity than a typical Duka;
- greater reporting and purchasing needs;
- management workflows become materially useful;
- mobile frontline operation plus PC/Web management where useful.

## 6.3 Growth

Canonical characteristics:

- sustained operational complexity;
- deeper inventory/procurement/reconciliation requirements;
- stronger staff controls and management reporting;
- greater need for automation and intelligence.

## 6.4 Multi-Branch

Canonical definition:

> **One organization operating two or more physical locations where centralized control is materially useful.**

A large single-location business is not automatically Multi-Branch.

Typical topology:

```text
                 CENTRAL CONTROL
                 PC / WEB
                     |
          +----------+----------+
          |          |          |
       BRANCH A   BRANCH B   BRANCH C
        MOBILE     MOBILE     MOBILE
```

## 6.5 Enterprise

Canonical definition:

> **An organization whose governance, authorization, audit, integration, security, procurement, SLA, or organizational complexity materially exceeds ordinary SME operation.**

Enterprise is not defined by PC ownership.

# 7. Device Consistency Audit

Device is a workflow property.

```text
MOBILE → FRONTLINE / TRANSACTIONAL OPERATION
PC     → MANAGEMENT / CONTROL
WEB    → ADMINISTRATION / CENTRAL CONTROL / SELECTED WORKFLOWS
```

Therefore:

- Duka is mobile-first, not permanently mobile-exclusive;
- SME and Growth can use mobile plus PC/Web according to job function;
- Multi-Branch normally uses mobile at branches and PC/Web centrally;
- Enterprise can use all surfaces according to organizational roles.

Device count MUST NOT become the commercial pricing unit merely because the product supports multiple devices.

If device-based pricing is ever introduced, it must be separately validated against customer value, willingness to pay, operational complexity, and contribution economics.

# 10. CAC Audit Contract

CAC MUST be segmented by acquisition motion where the costs differ materially.

Minimum dimensions:

- direct sales;
- paid acquisition;
- referral;
- partner;
- agent/distributor;
- assisted onboarding;
- enterprise sales.

Canonical formula:

```text
CAC = attributable acquisition + attributable sales/onboarding cost
      -----------------------------------------------------------
                    newly acquired paying customers
```

The denominator must be **new paying customers**, not:

- leads;
- registrations;
- downloads;
- demos;
- free accounts.

Where onboarding is channel-specific, attributable onboarding cost belongs in the channel CAC calculation unless the unit-economics document explicitly defines another approved treatment.

CAC payback must use contribution economics rather than gross subscription revenue when variable costs are material.

---

# 11. Contribution Audit

Revenue is not profit.

The corpus must distinguish:

```text
ARR / MRR
  ↓
VARIABLE COSTS
  ↓
CONTRIBUTION
  ↓
CAC PAYBACK
  ↓
FIXED COST / OVERHEAD
  ↓
OPERATING RESULT
```

At minimum, variable-cost analysis should consider where applicable:

- payment processing;
- communications;
- infrastructure attributable to usage;
- support attributable to customer activity;
- onboarding/implementation;
- partner/channel commissions;
- refunds/credits;
- integration-specific costs.

Enterprise implementation revenue must not be used to disguise recurring SaaS economics. One-time implementation, integration, migration, hardware, or professional-service economics should be separately identified.

---

# 12. Retention and Churn Audit

Commercial documents must distinguish:

```text
NOT ACTIVATED
≠
ACTIVATED THEN CHURNED
≠
SEASONALLY INACTIVE
≠
PAYMENT FAILURE
≠
VOLUNTARY CANCELLATION
≠
INVOLUNTARY SUSPENSION
≠
REACTIVATED
```

A merchant that has not transacted because its business is seasonal must not automatically be classified as product churn.

Retention cohorts must use explicit cohort dates and consistent observation windows.

A retention claim must specify:

- cohort definition;
- segment;
- activation definition;
- observation period;
- denominator;
- treatment of suspended/failed-payment accounts.

# 13. PMF Audit

PMF is not established by:

- downloads;
- registrations;
- interviews alone;
- demos;
- feature usage without value evidence;
- free trials without payment;
- one large customer;
- founder intuition.

PMF evidence must be segment-aware and should connect:

```text
REAL PAIN
→ REPEATED USE
→ PAYMENT
→ RETENTION
→ POSITIVE CONTRIBUTION
→ REPEATABLE ACQUISITION
```

Multi-branch and enterprise may additionally require:

```text
EXPANSION
→ MULTI-LOCATION ADOPTION
→ RENEWAL
→ GOVERNANCE / PROCUREMENT ACCEPTANCE
```

No document in the corpus may state that Sitolo has PMF merely because the validation framework exists.

# 14. Channel Audit

Every acquisition channel must eventually be evaluated on more than lead volume.

Minimum commercial dimensions:

| Dimension | Required question |
|---|---|
| Reach | can the channel reach the intended merchant population? |
| Conversion | does reach become paying customers? |
| CAC | what does a new paying customer cost? |
| Activation | do acquired customers actually use Sitolo? |
| Retention | do they remain active? |
| Contribution | does the customer generate positive contribution? |
| Capacity | can the channel scale operationally? |
| Fraud | can acquisition incentives be abused? |
| Attribution | can the acquisition source be measured reliably? |

A cheap lead source is not necessarily a cheap acquisition channel.

# 15. Duka-Specific Audit

The Duka segment exists because the addressable population includes very small neighborhood and rural businesses whose operations may rely on paper records, memory, informal processes, or no systematic records.

This is a strategic hypothesis, not a proven economic conclusion.

The Duka model therefore has a harder economic constraint than high-ARPU enterprise software:

```text
LOW ARPU
×
HIGH CUSTOMER COUNT
×
LOW ACQUISITION COST
×
LOW SUPPORT COST
×
HIGH RETENTION
```

The commercial audit must therefore reject any Duka strategy that assumes enterprise-style sales, onboarding, support, or implementation cost while retaining Duka-level pricing.

The required validation question is not merely:

> “Do Dukas want digital business software?”

It is:

> **“Can Sitolo acquire, activate, support, retain, and monetize a Duka at a cost structure that produces positive contribution?”**

# 17. Multi-Branch Audit

The defining commercial problem is centralized control across locations.

Required value dimensions include:

- branch visibility;
- consolidated reporting;
- centralized product/catalogue control where appropriate;
- role and authorization boundaries;
- inter-branch operational visibility;
- inventory and cash control;
- branch-level performance analysis;
- reduced management overhead.

A customer must not be forced into Multi-Branch pricing merely because it has a large catalogue, high revenue, or many employees if it operates one physical location and does not require branch control.

# 18. Enterprise Audit

Enterprise economics must remain distinct from SMB subscription economics.

Potential enterprise cost/revenue components include:

- procurement cycle;
- security review;
- implementation;
- migration;
- integration;
- training;
- contractual support;
- SLA requirements;
- account management;
- audit requirements;
- custom configuration.

Enterprise price must therefore be validated against the **whole cost-to-serve and value delivered**, not simply multiplied from the Duka price.

# 19. Regulatory Audit

Commercial strategy cannot redefine regulated activity.

Examples requiring explicit boundary control:

- lending/credit;
- payment services;
- stored value;
- tax/fiscal reporting;
- identity/KYC requirements;
- financial data handling.

The corpus must distinguish:

```text
RECORD / RECONCILE / REPORT
vs
PROVIDE A REGULATED FINANCIAL SERVICE
```

If a commercial proposal crosses the regulatory boundary, the relevant legal/regulatory authority and provider contracts take precedence.

---

# 20. Intelligence / AI Audit

Business intelligence and recommendations are value hypotheses.

The corpus must distinguish:

```text
OBSERVED RECORD
→ DERIVED METRIC
→ SIGNAL
→ RECOMMENDATION
```

from:

```text
PREDICTION
```

An alert does not prove an outcome.

The intelligence layer must not silently:

- change financial truth;
- mutate inventory;
- bypass approval;
- override authorization;
- alter tax state;
- create a regulated financial obligation.

Commercial value must ultimately be measured through customer outcomes, not “AI usage”.

# 22. Cross-Document Contradiction Tests

Before every commercial documentation release, run these tests.

### Test A — Tier contradiction

Search for every occurrence of:

- Duka;
- SME;
- Growth;
- Multi-Branch;
- Enterprise.

Verify that definitions do not conflict.

### Test B — Price contradiction

Search every documented price and verify:

- amount;
- currency;
- billing interval;
- tier;
- hypothesis status;
- source/authority.

### Test C — Device contradiction

Search for mobile, PC, Web, desktop and device-count statements.

Verify that device is never silently converted into the tier definition or pricing unit.

### Test D — CAC contradiction

Verify every CAC formula uses the canonical denominator and treatment of acquisition/onboarding/channel costs.

### Test E — Retention contradiction

Verify cohort and churn definitions use one canonical metric contract.

### Test F — PMF contradiction

Search for “PMF”, “product-market fit”, “validated”, “proven”, “traction”, and equivalent language.

Every positive claim must point to actual evidence or be clearly labelled as a gate/hypothesis.

### Test G — Regulatory contradiction

Verify commercial documents do not claim authority to provide regulated services where the regulatory corpus does not establish that authority.

### Test H — Scope contradiction

Verify that a validation cohort is never described as the permanent product scope unless an approved decision explicitly changes scope.

# 25. Implementation Boundary

Commercial documentation may establish:

- desired customer outcomes;
- pricing hypotheses;
- packaging hypotheses;
- workflow expectations;
- business constraints;
- commercial metrics.

It must not silently establish:

- database schema;
- API contracts;
- authorization implementation;
- cryptographic design;
- synchronization semantics;
- infrastructure topology;
- security controls.

Those belong to the relevant technical contracts.

A commercial requirement that materially changes technical architecture must create or update the appropriate technical design/ADR rather than embedding technical implementation into commercial prose.

# 26. Audit Severity Model

| Severity | Meaning | Required action |
|---|---|---|
| P0 | contradiction can cause materially wrong commercial or regulatory decision | block merge/release until resolved |
| P1 | material ambiguity affecting pricing, economics, validation or product scope | resolve before commercial decision |
| P2 | measurable inconsistency or missing traceability | resolve before next validation cycle |
| P3 | editorial/navigation issue | fix when practical |

Examples:

**P0**
- one document says Enterprise is defined by device ownership while another says organizational complexity;
- regulated lending is described as a normal Sitolo feature without the required regulatory basis.

**P1**
- two different CAC formulas;
- different prices for the same tier with no experiment/version distinction;
- different activation definitions.

**P2**
- experiment lacks explicit geography;
- metric has no owner;
- scenario has no assumption list.

**P3**
- broken internal link;
- inconsistent heading format.

The commercial corpus is eligible for a commercial-documentation release only when:

- [ ] every commercial document has an identified authority boundary;
- [ ] tier definitions are consistent;
- [ ] pricing values have explicit hypothesis status unless formally approved;
- [ ] device semantics are consistent;
- [ ] CAC formulas are consistent;
- [ ] contribution methodology is consistent;
- [ ] retention/churn definitions are consistent;
- [ ] the 12 validation experiments have one canonical registry;
- [ ] every experiment has measurable decision rules;
- [ ] PMF claims are evidence-backed or explicitly hypothetical;

- [ ] scenario, forecast, hypothesis and observation are not conflated;
- [ ] README/index navigation points to canonical documents;
- [ ] commercial changes with technical impact are linked to technical authority;
- [ ] unresolved P0/P1 contradictions are zero.

# 30. Final Contract

The Sitolo commercial documentation corpus is successful when it becomes **decision infrastructure**, not merely documentation.

The corpus must make it difficult to:

- accidentally change the meaning of a tier;
- present a planning price as a market fact;
- mistake a free user for a customer;
- mistake revenue for contribution;
- mistake registrations for PMF;
- mistake leads for CAC denominator customers;
- mistake a validation cohort for the permanent ICP;
- mistake device topology for customer segmentation;
- mistake a scenario for a forecast;
- mistake a recommendation for a prediction;
- mistake commercial intent for a technical requirement;
- mistake product capability for regulatory authorization.

The operating standard is:

> **Every commercially material claim must have a clear semantic class, an authority, a measurable validation path where applicable, and an explicit decision state.**

That is the minimum standard for treating the Sitolo commercial corpus as an enterprise-grade decision system.
